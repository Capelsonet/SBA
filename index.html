The commercial question
What if the client could see a leader in action before making a promotion or succession bet?

This is the core message that differentiates simulation-based assessment from test-plus-interview approaches. It reframes the offer from assessment as diagnosis to assessment as risk reduction.

For a client, the value is confidence. For a seller, the value is a much clearer bridge from methodology to business outcome.

What matters now
The page should lead with three promises: better prediction, clearer readiness signals, and stronger business performance after appointment. Everything else supports those claims.

Leadership thesis
The deck’s real intellectual center is Enterprise Leadership.
Core idea
Clients do not need more sophisticated executive managers. They need leaders who can transcend functional, geographic, and organizational boundaries in service of enterprise value creation.

This is the strategic language that elevates the offer above standard assessment. It tells the client that the problem is not just capability depth. It is enterprise-level judgment.

For the HTML page, this section should work as the conceptual bridge between evidence and offer. It explains why simulation matters: because enterprise leadership is hard to infer from interviews alone. It must be observed under tension, ambiguity, and competing stakeholder demands.

Offer logic
Simulation is persuasive because it lets the client observe future-role behavior, not infer it.

The deck compares business simulations to a flight simulator. That is a useful metaphor because it compresses the benefit into one idea: leaders are put into realistic role conditions before they are promoted into the role itself.

What the simulations do well ▼
They immerse participants in business situations, role-specific challenges, and strategic choices typical of the target level. The deck emphasizes that this goes beyond self-report and creates a more even playing field across talent populations.
Why the method matters commercially ▼
The more the client fears the cost of a wrong promotion or succession choice, the more simulation-based assessment becomes a risk-management product rather than an HR diagnostic.
How to position fairness and development ▼
The deck states that simulations create a fairer, more consistent playing field and lead to meaningful development insight. In a sales conversation, this lets the offer address both selection rigor and talent-development credibility.

Assessment portfolio
The level architecture helps a client see relevance to their own leadership pipeline.

The deck covers four target levels, each tied to a realistic leadership context. In the HTML, these should not feel like catalog items. They should show how Korn Ferry tailors simulation design to the strategic burden of the role.

CEO Assessment
Target role: CEO of a Global 1000 company with multiple products and services, spanning mature, high-growth, and turnaround or divestiture situations.
Senior Executive Assessment
Target role: President of the largest global business line inside a complex multi-business enterprise across B2B and B2C contexts.
Business Unit Leader Assessment
Target role: General Manager with full P&L ownership inside a global technology and communications context.
Mid-Level Leader Assessment
Target role: Mid-manager of a product line in a computer and services company, facing more bounded but still meaningful leadership tradeoffs.
Why this matters in the sale
The buyer should recognize their own population in the architecture. This section is less about inventory and more about helping the client see a pathway from critical roles to a fit-for-purpose assessment design.
Experience design
The delivery model should read as both rigorous and participant-centered.
Pre-assessment
Set context and prepare the participant
Kickoff communication, welcome call, pre-work, testing, questionnaire, and market interview where applicable.
Assessment
Observe the leader in action
Behavioral interview, business simulations, and in some cases real-time coaching around role-relevant dilemmas.
Post-assessment
Turn insight into action
Reporting, feedback and development session, and development planning meeting.
For the participant
A realistic preview of the role, development guidance, role-fit insight, and an actionable individual development plan tied to future challenges.
For the organization
Greater confidence in readiness decisions, richer data on leaders, more targeted development investment, faster onboarding, stronger engagement, and better retention.
